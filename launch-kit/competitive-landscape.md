# Mi Ciudadania - Competitors, threats, and opportunities

## 1. Strategic frame
Mi Ciudadania should not compete against attorneys. The product competes against confusion, procrastination, fragmented study resources, and avoidable filing mistakes.

The attorney relationship should be complementary:

- Routine, low-risk users get preparation software.
- Complex users are warned to seek legal review.
- Attorneys receive a cleaner, better-prepared user or a branded education tool.

## 2. Competitor map
1. Official government content: USCIS, USAGov
2. Legal service providers: immigration attorneys, legal marketplaces
3. Nonprofit citizenship programs
4. Civics-only study apps and websites
5. Generic immigration content publishers
6. Form-filling and document-prep services
7. Informal community advice: WhatsApp, YouTube, Facebook groups

## 3. Official content
Strengths:

- Highest trust
- Most authoritative
- Free

Weaknesses:

- Not designed as an end-to-end Spanish-first product workflow
- Does not personalize progress or confidence
- Does not create a commercial habit loop

Opportunity:

- Build on official content with clear source links and date-stamped updates.

## 4. Law firms
Strengths:

- Legal advice
- Case strategy
- Representation for risky facts

Weaknesses:

- Higher cost for routine users
- Capacity constraints
- Many routine users delay because they believe every case needs a lawyer

Opportunity:

- Partner, do not attack. Build attorney referral and co-branded education paths.

## 5. Nonprofits
Strengths:

- Trust
- Community presence
- Low-cost help

Weaknesses:

- Limited availability
- Geographic constraints
- Often event-based rather than always-on

Opportunity:

- Offer a lightweight digital prep layer that nonprofits can recommend between clinics.

## 6. Study-only apps
Strengths:

- Fast to understand
- Useful for civics memorization
- Often mobile-friendly

Weaknesses:

- Do not answer “am I ready to file?”
- Do not manage documents or timeline
- Do not simulate N-400 interview pressure

Opportunity:

- Own the integrated path: eligibility, roadmap, civics, interview.

## 7. Form services
Strengths:

- Transactional conversion
- User believes they are “getting it done”

Weaknesses:

- Risk of unauthorized-practice boundaries
- Can be perceived as opaque
- Often insufficient for readiness and study

Opportunity:

- Stay upstream: preparation and readiness before filing.

## 8. Biggest threats
- Unauthorized practice of law risk
- Outdated policy or fee information
- Trust deficit in immigration products
- Low willingness to pay if free content is “good enough”
- Acquisition cost in a broad immigrant market
- Platform imitation by larger legal-tech or study apps

## 9. Defensive choices
- Spanish-first product voice and UX
- Official-source links and visible update dates
- Legal stop-sign logic for risk facts
- Attorney advisory review before commercialization
- Local wedge before national spend
- High retention through progress, reminders, and practice streaks

## 10. Opportunities
- User-paid self-guided prep
- Attorney co-branded education portal
- Nonprofit or library group licenses
- Employer/community benefit for eligible permanent residents
- Bilingual interview practice packs
- Human-reviewed document checklist add-on without legal advice

## 11. MVP moat
The early moat is not technology alone. It is trust plus workflow design:

- Right language
- Right price
- Right warnings
- Right channel partners
- Right emotional tone for a high-stakes life event

## 12. Strategic recommendation
Launch as a paid validation experiment, not as a funded startup:

1. Keep the product narrow.
2. Make the disclaimer impossible to miss.
3. Charge early.
4. Track completion, not vanity traffic.
5. Recruit 5 attorney allies before scaling ads.
